# Future Technology card style (Boris's approved rules)

Every Future Technology card has:
- **name**: named the way real inventions are: mostly plain (nuclear clock, magnetic refrigerator, starch battery). Only about one in eight gets a memorable name, and only where it really lands (Starlight Compass, Weeping Lens). Never uniformly whimsical. (r16, revised Nov 16)
- **subtitle**: the plain description, at most 8 words, lowercase start ("a drone that steers by the stars"). (r16)
- **how**: two or three sentences a curious non-scientist follows without looking anything up. Sentence one is the problem it solves: why anyone would want it (r15). Then how the effect(s) do the job (r13). No jargon without a plain gloss.
- **obstacle**: one sentence, "what's stopping it": the single biggest thing between the lab and everyday life (r18). Be concrete and honest (cost, scale, a physics limit, safety, regulation, durability).
- It must matter: it would change daily life, save lives, or open a frontier. Clever but niche is out (r14).
- It may work by two or more effects, as long as at least one is new to its job (r17).
- Parts are existing board cards physically inside the device; fuel and whatever makes it stay out (r9). At least one part (r12).
- If a part already does the job by the same effect, name the narrower effect that is actually new (r11).
- Reject effects too weak to do the job at a useful size (r5).
- Not an improvement: if today's tools already do the job well enough, it must change who can do it, where, or at what cost (no power, no expert, a fraction of the price). A better part for an existing device doesn't count (r19, Nov 18).

Two examples Boris liked the style of:

Starlight Compass · a drone that steers by the stars
how: GPS gets jammed and spoofed in war zones, and a drone's own motors throw off a magnetic compass. A small upward-facing camera matches the stars it sees against a star map, and since the pole star holds still while the rest wheel around it, the drone always knows which way is north.
obstacle: Daytime: the sky is too bright for a cheap camera to see stars, so for now it only works at night or above the clouds.

Weeping Lens · a contact lens that treats inflammation as it happens
how: Eye drops wash away in minutes, so inflamed eyes get treated in bursts. This lens holds cortisone in tiny capsules, and lysozyme, the germ-killing enzyme in tears, slowly eats through their walls, so a sore, teary eye gets more medicine; the drug then seeps into the eye through a membrane.
effects: cortisone calms inflammation; an enzyme in tears breaks open bacteria; small molecules seep through a membrane.
obstacle: Proving that the dose stays steady for weeks while the lens stays clear and comfortable.
